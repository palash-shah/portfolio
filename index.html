I make markets that trade probabilities behave like markets you can actually hedge.

I'm Palash Shah, a student at Carnegie Mellon University working at the intersection of mathematical finance, machine learning, and market design.

My most recent paper, Terminal Pinning in Prediction Markets, models how event-contract prices collapse to {0,1} at resolution through information-driven Brownian bridges, with implied-volatility asymptotics validated on 50 resolved Polymarket markets totalling $6.4B in volume. It sits atop my broader Option-Implied Event Contracts framework: closed-form option pricing on bounded underlyings, a full Greeks calculus, variance swaps on beliefs, and the margin methodology an exchange would need to clear these products.

The theorems don't stay on paper. Each framework here is paired with working systems: pricing engines, calibrators, market-making simulators, hedging engines. Alongside, I research implied volatility surfaces through spectral clustering, LSTM forecasting, and reinforcement learning.

Terminal Pinning in Prediction Markets: Option Pricing Near Event Resolution via Information-Driven Bridge Dynamics

Palash Shah, Carnegie Mellon · 29 pages

An information-based framework where pinning to {0,1} emerges endogenously: the price is a martingale mixture of two Brownian bridges. Sharp implied-volatility asymptotics, a completeness theorem, and empirical validation on 50 resolved Polymarket markets totalling $6.4B in volume.
